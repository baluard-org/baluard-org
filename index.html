A password manager that answers to you, not to a server.

Baluard keeps your passwords on your devices. No account. No cloud. No subscription standing between you and your data. It works offline today and it will keep working offline in ten years, because everything it needs is already on your machine.

Prefer your package manager? (Ubuntu, Debian, Fedora)

Install from the Baluard repository and updates arrive with your usual system updates. Ubuntu and Debian:

curl -fsSL https://baluard.app/repo/baluard-release.asc | sudo gpg --dearmor -o /usr/share/keyrings/baluard-archive-keyring.gpg
echo "deb [signed-by=/usr/share/keyrings/baluard-archive-keyring.gpg] https://baluard.app/repo stable main" | sudo tee /etc/apt/sources.list.d/baluard.list
sudo apt update && sudo apt install baluard

Fedora:

sudo tee /etc/yum.repos.d/baluard.repo <<'EOF'
[baluard]
name=Baluard
baseurl=https://baluard.app/repo/rpms
enabled=1
gpgcheck=1
repo_gpgcheck=1
gpgkey=https://baluard.app/repo/baluard-release.asc
EOF
sudo dnf install baluard

The repository signing key fingerprint is 223310EA BF1A B6FE E6BD DF3E 2A53 1D54 E6D7 4804. Your system shows it on first use; check it before trusting the repository.

Security

Baluard is built on a simple rule: your secrets are encrypted on your device, and the keys to open them never leave it. There is no server to breach, no database of everyone's passwords, no breach headline waiting to happen.

Only your master password can open your vault

It is never sent anywhere, because there is nowhere to send it. Everything inside is encrypted before it touches the disk.

A hardware key can seal the whole vault

Enroll a YubiKey and your vault refuses to open without a physical tap, even if someone learns your master password.

The whole design is written down

Read the security white paper: how the encryption works, what Baluard protects against, and where the honest limits are.
